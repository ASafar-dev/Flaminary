01. Welcome and purpose (38.543 seconds)
Flaminary is a daily creativity app for beginner and intermediate drummers. The idea is simple: a small restriction can help you discover a different groove or fill. Instead of wondering what to practise, you get one focused prompt, with allowed kit pieces, a tempo, a time signature, and a suggested duration. The name combines the drum rudiment flam with imaginary. This demonstration uses the Expo web app at a mobile screen size. The welcome screen introduces that purpose and leads to the mock login.

02. Login and validation (38.263 seconds)
The login screen uses the same validation approach as the class examples. Zod checks the email format and password length before the app compares the entered values with its predefined credentials. Invalid input stays in the form with readable feedback. The demo email is student at example dot com. The password is the word password, followed by the digits two, zero, two, six. A valid login opens the main screens. The signed-in session is local, and passwords are not saved with the challenge data.

03. Daily prompt and details (42.806 seconds)
Today is the starting point for a practice session. Its featured card shows the assigned challenge, difficulty, musical restriction, tempo, time signature, and suggested minutes. The prompt stays stable during the same local calendar day. Opening it reveals the full idea, a highlighted drum kit, and three practice steps. The kit illustration makes the allowed instruments easy to identify. These limits are an invitation to experiment, and the player chooses what to play inside them. The app supplies the prompt and leaves the musical decisions to the drummer.

04. Archive search and filters (37.864 seconds)
Explore is the challenge archive. Search checks titles, descriptions, restrictions, difficulty, and kit pieces. Category filters narrow it to grooves or fills. Practice filters show ideas to try, completed challenges, or custom ideas. These controls work together, and the visible result count updates. No matches produces a clear message with a reset action. Every card opens its current details, and the archive also offers challenge creation.

05. Create and editor validation (42.786 seconds)
The editor handles both adding and editing. It asks for a title, a description, a type, a difficulty, allowed kit pieces, and a creative restriction. Tempo, time signature, and suggested duration keep the prompt practical. An incomplete submission shows field errors and preserves the entered values. At least one kit piece is required. Tempo must be a whole number between forty and two hundred forty, and duration is five to forty-five minutes. After valid input is saved, the new challenge opens in Details and joins the shared collection.

06. Edit completion and progress (38.693 seconds)
Editing an existing challenge updates that same item. There is no separate copy to get out of sync. Cancelling the form leaves the saved version unchanged. The completion action records a self-reported finish date, and it can be undone. Progress lists completed challenges and derives its counts from the collection. It also shows the number of custom ideas and the suggested minutes attached to completed challenges. Those minutes describe planned practice duration; they are not a measurement from a running timer.

07. Persistence and confirmed deletion (38.243 seconds)
Async Storage keeps challenges, completion records, and daily assignments between restarts. Reloading requires the mock login again, while practice data remains. Saved empty collections stay empty, too. Loading and saving have visible feedback, and failed reads preserve the saved library. Deletion is deliberate: the dialog offers cancellation and confirmation. Cancelling keeps the item. Confirming removes it and its completion record, with the change reflected throughout the app.

08. Logout credits and testing limits (35.879 seconds)
Logging out removes access to signed-in screens, including back navigation.
